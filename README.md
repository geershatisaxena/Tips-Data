### ✅ Step 3 — Outlier Detection (IQR Method)

```python
# ─── IQR-Based Outlier Flagging ───────────────────────
for col in ["total_bill", "tip"]:
    Q1  = tips[col].quantile(0.25)
    Q3  = tips[col].quantile(0.75)
    IQR = Q3 - Q1
    lo  = Q1 - 1.5 * IQR
    hi  = Q3 + 1.5 * IQR

    outs = tips[(tips[col] < lo) | (tips[col] > hi)]
    print(f"📦 {col:12s} | Q1={Q1:.2f} Q3={Q3:.2f} "
          f"IQR={IQR:.2f} | Bounds=[{lo:.2f}, {hi:.2f}] "
          f"| 🚨 Outliers: {len(outs)}")
```

```
📦 total_bill  | Q1=13.35  Q3=24.13  IQR=10.78 | Bounds=[-2.82, 40.30] | 🚨 Outliers: 9
📦 tip         | Q1=2.00   Q3=3.56   IQR=1.56  | Bounds=[-0.34, 5.90]  | 🚨 Outliers: 11
```

## 💡 Key Insights & Findings

<img src="https://readme-typing-svg.demolab.com?font=Orbitron&weight=700&size=16&pause=2000&color=FFD700&center=false&vCenter=true&width=700&lines=🔬+What+244+Restaurant+Bills+Reveal+About+Human+Behavior..." />

---

> ### 💰 Insight 1 — The Bill-Tip Iron Law
> **Correlation r = +0.68** — The single strongest predictor of tip size is total bill. Every $10 increase in the bill corresponds to approximately **$1.34 more in tips**. Upselling food and drinks directly grows tip revenue for waitstaff.

---

> ### 🍽️ Insight 2 — Dinner Rules Everything
> Dinner generates **~33% higher median bills** than Lunch ($20 vs $15). Friday dinner shows the highest tip percentages, possibly because it marks the start of the weekend. Prioritizing dinner service quality delivers disproportionate revenue impact.

---

> ### 📅 Insight 3 — The Weekend Effect
> Saturday + Sunday account for **66.8% of all visits** (163/244). Saturday alone drives 35.7% of weekly covers. Weekend diners also show broader spending variance — a mix of family groups and celebratory parties pushing bills higher.

---

> ### 👥 Insight 4 — Groups Are Gold
> Party size and total bill show a **+0.60 correlation**. Each additional guest adds an estimated **$5.14** to the average bill. Tables of 5–6 generate the restaurant's highest per-visit revenue, yet account for only ~8% of all visits — a major upsell opportunity.

---

> ### 📐 Insight 5 — The 20% Tip Barrier
> Only **~22% of customers** tip at or above the culturally expected 20% standard. The average tip percentage is **~16%**. Non-standard tipping is overwhelmingly common, suggesting that automatic gratuity policies for large groups could meaningfully increase staff earnings.

---

> ### 🚬 Insight 6 — Smoking Is a Weak Signal
> Despite having 93 observations, smoker status shows **near-zero predictive correlation** with tip percentage (r=−0.03). It should not be used as a segmentation or service-prioritization variable. Focus instead on bill size and party size.

---

> ### 👤 Insight 7 — Gender Gap Is Overstated
> The $0.26 average tip difference between males and females almost entirely disappears after **controlling for bill size**. Male customers simply tend to have higher bills (due to picking up group tabs), not intrinsically different tipping behavior.

---

> ### 🌟 Insight 8 — High Tippers Are Rare but Valuable
> The top 10% of tippers (tip ≥ $5) account for a disproportionate share of tip revenue. These customers skew toward **Friday dinner, party sizes of 2–3, and non-smoker status**. Identifying and rewarding this segment could drive loyalty.
